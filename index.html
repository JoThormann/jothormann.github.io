01 Software · on the screen by the pump

Feed Profile Designer

Puts a fermentation plan on a real calendar, before the run starts.

  • One weekOne process, planned across the working week
  • No installFree, and runs locally in your browser
  • Substrate useEstimated before the run starts

Open the designer → or watch it work ↓

How it works

A feed profile is planned on paper and executed on a clock. A phase switch that looks reasonable in a spreadsheet can land at 3am, which you tend to discover once the run is already going.

Set the feed phases: constant, linear ramp, exponential, each with its own temperature and substrate concentration, and it returns the pump setpoints, shows the effective substrate concentrations in the vessel, and shows where every change in the process falls in the week. A goal-seek solver can help make adjustments and move changes into the working hours. Expert mode gives you OUR and specific growth rate information.

  • All common vesselsPCR tubes to 50 ml, vials and cuvettes, or your own size

On the bench

Open the designer → or take the tour ↓

How it works

Racks come in the sizes a catalogue has. The one that fits this water bath, this ice bucket or this drawer, for the tubes actually in use, usually has to be made.

Pick the vessel, set how many across and deep, and choose a solid block, an open stand, a tray or a stand for a single tube. Every size can be changed, and extras such as viewing slots, dividers, numbers and stacking feet are one setting each. The tool spaces the openings so that caps clear each other, warns about thin walls, tipping and a print bed that is too small, and can fill a box of a given size. It picks which way up to print, turns sideways holes into teardrops, and exports the part as 3MF or STL. A design is shared as a link.

04 Hardware · on the vent line

DIY off-gas sensor

Reads what a bioreactor breathes out, continuously, with parts you can easily order.

  • About €500In parts you can order
  • OUR · CER · RQOxygen uptake, CO₂ production and their ratio
  • CSVEvery reading, timestamped, over USB

Build instructions and print files are in preparation.

How it works

Every run vents a continuous record of its own metabolism. Reading it normally means an analyser around €6,500, which is why it often goes unread.

An O₂ cell and a CO₂ cell sit in the vent line, a BME280 corrects both to dry gas, and a Teensy 4.0 streams all three to a timestamped CSV. The cells ship with threads and no mounts, so the Y adapter connecting them is printed with an SLA resin 3D printer.
